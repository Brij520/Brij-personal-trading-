# 05 · Journal discipline

The journal is the only part of this system that improves with use. Everything else computes;
this records whether *you* are the problem.

## What to record, every time

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Entry, stop, target | Reconstructs the initial R. Without it, a "win" cannot be judged. |
| Setup name | Lets the strategy breakdown tell you which of your ideas actually work. |
| Invalidation sentence | The only record of what you believed before the outcome was known. |
| Size | To catch "I risked 2% but the position was 40% of capital". |
| Mistakes (tagged) | FOMO, moved stop, oversized, revenge trade, ignored the report. |

## The weekly review, honestly read

Run **Journal → weekly review**. Then read these four, in this order:

1. **Win rate is not the headline.** With fewer than 20 trades it is not a statistic at all;
   the platform labels it as such. Read expectancy and average R instead.
2. **Mistake counts beat P&L.** Three profitable trades tagged `moved_stop` is a bad week that
   got lucky.
3. **Discipline checks** flag trades opened after a daily-loss halt, and positions sized above
   the limit. These are process failures even when they made money.
4. **Which setups?** The strategy breakdown is how you discover that your "breakout" ideas lose
   and your "pullback" ideas win — the finding you cannot get from memory.

## The rules that follow from the journal

- **Every proposal in the review is `approved_automatically: false`.** The platform will not
  tell you to change your plan, because it cannot know whether one bad week is noise. You make
  that call — but you make it with the counts in front of you, once a week, not mid-trade.
- **You do not change anything until the sample is 20+ closed trades.** Changing a rule after
  three losses guarantees you never learn whether the rule worked.
- **You do not delete a losing trade.** It is the only data point that cost real money to
  obtain.

## The monthly question

One question, written down: *which single behaviour cost me the most this month?* Not which
stock, not which sector — which behaviour. Then fix that one thing for the next month. One
behaviour per month is the maximum a human being changes reliably.
